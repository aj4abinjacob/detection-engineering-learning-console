Purpose

Why this matters in detection engineering

At-least-once delivery means a consumer must tolerate the same logical event more than once. An idempotency key protects the durable side effect; retries need classification, backoff, attempt limits, and terminal handling. A dead-letter queue is an investigation queue, not a disposal bin.

A detection engineer must be able to trace how an observation becomes a feature, how that feature supports an inference, and how policy turns the inference into an action. The useful unit for this topic is a correctness matrix covering duplicate, transient retry, retry exhaustion, poison payload, consumer crash, and out-of-order delivery. Preserve enough lineage to reproduce it without collecting content that the decision does not require.

Boundaries

What can go wrong

Closest legitimate lookalike: a valid message may repeatedly fail because a dependency is unavailable rather than because its content is poison.

Pipeline or interpretation failure: non-atomic dedup and effect, infinite requeue, shared retry counters, sensitive DLQ payloads, and silent discard cause repeated harm or loss.

Safety boundary: work only with supplied sanitized fixtures or systems and accounts you own and are authorized to test. Minimize identifiers, exclude secrets and content, bound retention and access, and provide an accessible path when an automated action could affect a person.

Do not overclaimThis guide establishes a method for controlled defensive evidence. It does not prove identity, intent, causality, universal tool behavior, or how a third-party system will classify traffic.

Method

A repeatable defensive workflow

  1. Declare the question and scope. Name the owned route, entity, time range, authorization, expected population, and prohibited data before collection.
  2. Version the evidence. Save a correctness matrix covering duplicate, transient retry, retry exhaustion, poison payload, consumer crash, and out-of-order delivery, its schema or tool version, observation point, event and availability times, and missing-value behavior.
  3. Build a negative control. Include a valid message may repeatedly fail because a dependency is unavailable rather than because its content is poison and state the response it should receive.
  4. Replay and decide. Recompute from immutable input, compare expected output, inspect false positives and false negatives, then apply commit idempotency and result atomically, cap retries, preserve redacted failure context, alert on DLQ age, and provide controlled replay.

Practice

Detection-engineering scenarios

BASELINE

1. The observed distribution changes

Trigger: a monitor for Idempotency keys; retries with limits shifts outside its recorded baseline. Artifact: a correctness matrix covering duplicate, transient retry, retry exhaustion, poison payload, consumer crash, and out-of-order delivery. Reasoning: compare time, producer, release, population, and data quality before attributing the shift to abuse. Boundary: a changed distribution is an observed fact, not a malicious label.

LOOKALIKE

2. A legitimate journey matches the candidate

Trigger: the candidate logic fires on a valid message may repeatedly fail because a dependency is unavailable rather than because its content is poison. Artifact: a labeled negative-control replay. Reasoning: preserve the case, inspect which feature dominated, and choose a proportionate fallback. Boundary: do not tune the case away without measuring what new false negatives the change creates.

QUALITY

3. The pipeline changes while the rule stays green

Trigger: non-atomic dedup and effect, infinite requeue, shared retry counters, sensitive DLQ payloads, and silent discard cause repeated harm or loss. Artifact: contract-test result, schema versions, and quality counters. Reasoning: quarantine or mark degraded evidence and restore the producer contract before judging detection quality. Boundary: absence, default values, and parser failures are not benign observations.

POLICY

4. Evidence is strong enough to review, not to punish

Trigger: several versioned signals agree on an unusual owned-lab journey. Artifact: feature lineage, reason codes, policy version, and expected guardrails. Reasoning: commit idempotency and result atomically, cap retries, preserve redacted failure context, alert on DLQ age, and provide controlled replay. Boundary: preserve rollback and never infer that an offline score proves the causal benefit of an intervention.
