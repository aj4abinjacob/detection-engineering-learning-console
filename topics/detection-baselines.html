Purpose

Why this matters in detection engineering

A baseline is the simplest credible alternative a candidate must beat under the same data, split, threshold-selection, and cost assumptions. Always-majority, a rate threshold, and the current rule set expose whether complexity adds operational value.

A detection engineer must be able to trace how an observation becomes a feature, how that feature supports an inference, and how policy turns the inference into an action. The useful unit for this topic is a baseline comparison table with identical held-out rows, confusion matrices, latency, coverage, cost assumptions, and uncertainty. Preserve enough lineage to reproduce it without collecting content that the decision does not require.

Boundaries

What can go wrong

Closest legitimate lookalike: a simple rule can outperform a model on a stable narrow route and should not be dismissed as unsophisticated.

Pipeline or interpretation failure: different splits, tuned candidate thresholds, untuned baselines, or ignoring abstentions and missing coverage manufacture improvement.

Safety boundary: work only with supplied sanitized fixtures or systems and accounts you own and are authorized to test. Minimize identifiers, exclude secrets and content, bound retention and access, and provide an accessible path when an automated action could affect a person.

Do not overclaimThis guide establishes a method for controlled defensive evidence. It does not prove identity, intent, causality, universal tool behavior, or how a third-party system will classify traffic.

Method

A repeatable defensive workflow

  1. Declare the question and scope. Name the owned route, entity, time range, authorization, expected population, and prohibited data before collection.
  2. Version the evidence. Save a baseline comparison table with identical held-out rows, confusion matrices, latency, coverage, cost assumptions, and uncertainty, its schema or tool version, observation point, event and availability times, and missing-value behavior.
  3. Build a negative control. Include a simple rule can outperform a model on a stable narrow route and should not be dismissed as unsophisticated and state the response it should receive.
  4. Replay and decide. Recompute from immutable input, compare expected output, inspect false positives and false negatives, then apply ship only when the candidate produces a meaningful cost-adjusted gain and remains reproducible; otherwise keep the baseline.

Practice

Detection-engineering scenarios

BASELINE

1. The observed distribution changes

Trigger: a monitor for Always-majority; rate-threshold; current rules shifts outside its recorded baseline. Artifact: a baseline comparison table with identical held-out rows, confusion matrices, latency, coverage, cost assumptions, and uncertainty. Reasoning: compare time, producer, release, population, and data quality before attributing the shift to abuse. Boundary: a changed distribution is an observed fact, not a malicious label.

LOOKALIKE

2. A legitimate journey matches the candidate

Trigger: the candidate logic fires on a simple rule can outperform a model on a stable narrow route and should not be dismissed as unsophisticated. Artifact: a labeled negative-control replay. Reasoning: preserve the case, inspect which feature dominated, and choose a proportionate fallback. Boundary: do not tune the case away without measuring what new false negatives the change creates.

QUALITY

3. The pipeline changes while the rule stays green

Trigger: different splits, tuned candidate thresholds, untuned baselines, or ignoring abstentions and missing coverage manufacture improvement. Artifact: contract-test result, schema versions, and quality counters. Reasoning: quarantine or mark degraded evidence and restore the producer contract before judging detection quality. Boundary: absence, default values, and parser failures are not benign observations.

POLICY

4. Evidence is strong enough to review, not to punish

Trigger: several versioned signals agree on an unusual owned-lab journey. Artifact: feature lineage, reason codes, policy version, and expected guardrails. Reasoning: ship only when the candidate produces a meaningful cost-adjusted gain and remains reproducible; otherwise keep the baseline. Boundary: preserve rollback and never infer that an offline score proves the causal benefit of an intervention.
