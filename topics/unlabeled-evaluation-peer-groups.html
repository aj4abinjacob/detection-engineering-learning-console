Purpose

Why this matters in detection engineering

Without complete labels, evaluation measures the quality of a discovery process: reviewed precision at K, capture of planted or known cases, stability, diversity, analyst yield, and time to useful hypothesis. Peer groups make comparison local but can encode harmful or unstable segmentation.

A detection engineer must be able to trace how an observation becomes a feature, how that feature supports an inference, and how policy turns the inference into an action. The useful unit for this topic is a review manifest with immutable ranking, budget K, sampling rule, peer-group definition, reviewer outcome, uncertainty, and missed planted cases. Preserve enough lineage to reproduce it without collecting content that the decision does not require.

Boundaries

What can go wrong

Closest legitimate lookalike: small or new peer groups, legitimate specialists, and seasonal behavior can make ordinary activity look locally rare.

Pipeline or interpretation failure: changing K after review, leaking planted-case markers, defining peers from protected or proxy attributes, and ignoring unreviewed candidates bias conclusions.

Safety boundary: work only with supplied sanitized fixtures or systems and accounts you own and are authorized to test. Minimize identifiers, exclude secrets and content, bound retention and access, and provide an accessible path when an automated action could affect a person.

Do not overclaimThis guide establishes a method for controlled defensive evidence. It does not prove identity, intent, causality, universal tool behavior, or how a third-party system will classify traffic.

Method

A repeatable defensive workflow

  1. Declare the question and scope. Name the owned route, entity, time range, authorization, expected population, and prohibited data before collection.
  2. Version the evidence. Save a review manifest with immutable ranking, budget K, sampling rule, peer-group definition, reviewer outcome, uncertainty, and missed planted cases, its schema or tool version, observation point, event and availability times, and missing-value behavior.
  3. Build a negative control. Include small or new peer groups, legitimate specialists, and seasonal behavior can make ordinary activity look locally rare and state the response it should receive.
  4. Replay and decide. Recompute from immutable input, compare expected output, inspect false positives and false negatives, then apply pre-register review and peer rules, sample outside the top K, report what remains unknown, and use results for discovery rather than blocking.

Detection-engineering scenarios

BASELINE

1. The observed distribution changes

Trigger: a monitor for How to judge an unsupervised detector when you have no ground truth shifts outside its recorded baseline. Artifact: a review manifest with immutable ranking, budget K, sampling rule, peer-group definition, reviewer outcome, uncertainty, and missed planted cases. Reasoning: compare time, producer, release, population, and data quality before attributing the shift to abuse. Boundary: a changed distribution is an observed fact, not a malicious label.

LOOKALIKE

2. A legitimate journey matches the candidate

Trigger: the candidate logic fires on small or new peer groups, legitimate specialists, and seasonal behavior can make ordinary activity look locally rare. Artifact: a labeled negative-control replay. Reasoning: preserve the case, inspect which feature dominated, and choose a proportionate fallback. Boundary: do not tune the case away without measuring what new false negatives the change creates.

QUALITY

3. The pipeline changes while the rule stays green

Trigger: changing K after review, leaking planted-case markers, defining peers from protected or proxy attributes, and ignoring unreviewed candidates bias conclusions. Artifact: contract-test result, schema versions, and quality counters. Reasoning: quarantine or mark degraded evidence and restore the producer contract before judging detection quality. Boundary: absence, default values, and parser failures are not benign observations.

POLICY

4. Evidence is strong enough to review, not to punish

Trigger: several versioned signals agree on an unusual owned-lab journey. Artifact: feature lineage, reason codes, policy version, and expected guardrails. Reasoning: pre-register review and peer rules, sample outside the top K, report what remains unknown, and use results for discovery rather than blocking. Boundary: preserve rollback and never infer that an offline score proves the causal benefit of an intervention.
