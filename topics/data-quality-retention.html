Purpose

Why this matters in detection engineering

Data quality describes whether telemetry is complete, valid, timely, unique, and semantically stable enough for its intended decision. Retention describes when each copy is aggregated, deleted, or placed under an approved hold.

A detection engineer must be able to trace how an observation becomes a feature, how that feature supports an inference, and how policy turns the inference into an action. The useful unit for this topic is quality counters and a request-to-decision trace with event time, observed time, validity, dedup status, storage tier, and expiry. Preserve enough lineage to reproduce it without collecting content that the decision does not require.

Boundaries

What can go wrong

Closest legitimate lookalike: planned producer rollout, temporary backfill, and delayed mobile delivery may move quality counters without an attack.

Pipeline or interpretation failure: silent drops, retry duplicates, clock skew, schema coercion, and forgotten derived tables make a detector confidently wrong.

Safety boundary: work only with supplied sanitized fixtures or systems and accounts you own and are authorized to test. Minimize identifiers, exclude secrets and content, bound retention and access, and provide an accessible path when an automated action could affect a person.

Do not overclaimThis guide establishes a method for controlled defensive evidence. It does not prove identity, intent, causality, universal tool behavior, or how a third-party system will classify traffic.

Method

A repeatable defensive workflow

  1. Declare the question and scope. Name the owned route, entity, time range, authorization, expected population, and prohibited data before collection.
  2. Version the evidence. Save quality counters and a request-to-decision trace with event time, observed time, validity, dedup status, storage tier, and expiry, its schema or tool version, observation point, event and availability times, and missing-value behavior.
  3. Build a negative control. Include planned producer rollout, temporary backfill, and delayed mobile delivery may move quality counters without an attack and state the response it should receive.
  4. Replay and decide. Recompute from immutable input, compare expected output, inspect false positives and false negatives, then apply alert on quality budgets separately from abuse, fail safe for critical missing fields, and verify deletion across derived stores.

Practice

Detection-engineering scenarios

BASELINE

1. The observed distribution changes

Trigger: a monitor for Counters: missing/invalid/late/duplicate shifts outside its recorded baseline. Artifact: quality counters and a request-to-decision trace with event time, observed time, validity, dedup status, storage tier, and expiry. Reasoning: compare time, producer, release, population, and data quality before attributing the shift to abuse. Boundary: a changed distribution is an observed fact, not a malicious label.

LOOKALIKE

2. A legitimate journey matches the candidate

Trigger: the candidate logic fires on planned producer rollout, temporary backfill, and delayed mobile delivery may move quality counters without an attack. Artifact: a labeled negative-control replay. Reasoning: preserve the case, inspect which feature dominated, and choose a proportionate fallback. Boundary: do not tune the case away without measuring what new false negatives the change creates.

QUALITY

3. The pipeline changes while the rule stays green

Trigger: silent drops, retry duplicates, clock skew, schema coercion, and forgotten derived tables make a detector confidently wrong. Artifact: contract-test result, schema versions, and quality counters. Reasoning: quarantine or mark degraded evidence and restore the producer contract before judging detection quality. Boundary: absence, default values, and parser failures are not benign observations.

POLICY

4. Evidence is strong enough to review, not to punish

Trigger: several versioned signals agree on an unusual owned-lab journey. Artifact: feature lineage, reason codes, policy version, and expected guardrails. Reasoning: alert on quality budgets separately from abuse, fail safe for critical missing fields, and verify deletion across derived stores. Boundary: preserve rollback and never infer that an offline score proves the causal benefit of an intervention.
