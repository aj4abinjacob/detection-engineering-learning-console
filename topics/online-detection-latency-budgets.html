Purpose

Why this matters in detection engineering

A latency budget allocates an end-to-end decision deadline across queueing, feature reads, rules or model work, policy, and network overhead. Percentiles describe a distribution; p99 is the value at or below which 99 percent of observed samples fall under the declared window and population.

A detection engineer must be able to trace how an observation becomes a feature, how that feature supports an inference, and how policy turns the inference into an action. The useful unit for this topic is a per-stage latency trace with clock source, sampling period, warm/cold distinction, timeout, fallback, p50/p95/p99, and SLO. Preserve enough lineage to reproduce it without collecting content that the decision does not require.

Boundaries

What can go wrong

Closest legitimate lookalike: cold starts, cache misses, regional paths, and legitimate high-cardinality entities can populate the tail.

Pipeline or interpretation failure: averages, coordinated omission, client/server clock confusion, unlimited fan-out, and retries outside the budget hide user impact.

Safety boundary: work only with supplied sanitized fixtures or systems and accounts you own and are authorized to test. Minimize identifiers, exclude secrets and content, bound retention and access, and provide an accessible path when an automated action could affect a person.

Do not overclaimThis guide establishes a method for controlled defensive evidence. It does not prove identity, intent, causality, universal tool behavior, or how a third-party system will classify traffic.

Method

A repeatable defensive workflow

  1. Declare the question and scope. Name the owned route, entity, time range, authorization, expected population, and prohibited data before collection.
  2. Version the evidence. Save a per-stage latency trace with clock source, sampling period, warm/cold distinction, timeout, fallback, p50/p95/p99, and SLO, its schema or tool version, observation point, event and availability times, and missing-value behavior.
  3. Build a negative control. Include cold starts, cache misses, regional paths, and legitimate high-cardinality entities can populate the tail and state the response it should receive.
  4. Replay and decide. Recompute from immutable input, compare expected output, inspect false positives and false negatives, then apply bound every dependency, shed optional work before the deadline, choose a safe degraded result, and monitor end-to-end plus per-stage percentiles.

Practice

Detection-engineering scenarios

BASELINE

1. The observed distribution changes

Trigger: a monitor for Online decision path; p99; bounded work shifts outside its recorded baseline. Artifact: a per-stage latency trace with clock source, sampling period, warm/cold distinction, timeout, fallback, p50/p95/p99, and SLO. Reasoning: compare time, producer, release, population, and data quality before attributing the shift to abuse. Boundary: a changed distribution is an observed fact, not a malicious label.

LOOKALIKE

2. A legitimate journey matches the candidate

Trigger: the candidate logic fires on cold starts, cache misses, regional paths, and legitimate high-cardinality entities can populate the tail. Artifact: a labeled negative-control replay. Reasoning: preserve the case, inspect which feature dominated, and choose a proportionate fallback. Boundary: do not tune the case away without measuring what new false negatives the change creates.

QUALITY

3. The pipeline changes while the rule stays green

Trigger: averages, coordinated omission, client/server clock confusion, unlimited fan-out, and retries outside the budget hide user impact. Artifact: contract-test result, schema versions, and quality counters. Reasoning: quarantine or mark degraded evidence and restore the producer contract before judging detection quality. Boundary: absence, default values, and parser failures are not benign observations.

POLICY

4. Evidence is strong enough to review, not to punish

Trigger: several versioned signals agree on an unusual owned-lab journey. Artifact: feature lineage, reason codes, policy version, and expected guardrails. Reasoning: bound every dependency, shed optional work before the deadline, choose a safe degraded result, and monitor end-to-end plus per-stage percentiles. Boundary: preserve rollback and never infer that an offline score proves the causal benefit of an intervention.
