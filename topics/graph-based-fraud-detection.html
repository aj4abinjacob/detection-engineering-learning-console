Purpose

Why this matters in detection engineering

Graph detection represents typed entities as nodes and confidence-scored temporal relationships as edges. Connected components and ring features prioritize structures for review; density alone cannot distinguish coordinated fraud from a household, office, carrier NAT, or popular payment service.

A detection engineer must be able to trace how an observation becomes a feature, how that feature supports an inference, and how policy turns the inference into an action. The useful unit for this topic is a sanitized graph with typed nodes, temporal/confidence edges, planted rings, planted legitimate shared-identifier components, ranked candidates, and review labels. Preserve enough lineage to reproduce it without collecting content that the decision does not require.

Boundaries

What can go wrong

Closest legitimate lookalike: households, corporate networks, shared devices, merchants, schools, and public Wi-Fi form dense legitimate components.

Pipeline or interpretation failure: unconditional entity merges, timeless edges, degree leakage, reviewing only planted rings, and blocking an entire component turn correlation into collective punishment.

Safety boundary: work only with supplied sanitized fixtures or systems and accounts you own and are authorized to test. Minimize identifiers, exclude secrets and content, bound retention and access, and provide an accessible path when an automated action could affect a person.

Do not overclaimThis guide establishes a method for controlled defensive evidence. It does not prove identity, intent, causality, universal tool behavior, or how a third-party system will classify traffic.

Method

A repeatable defensive workflow

  1. Declare the question and scope. Name the owned route, entity, time range, authorization, expected population, and prohibited data before collection.
  2. Version the evidence. Save a sanitized graph with typed nodes, temporal/confidence edges, planted rings, planted legitimate shared-identifier components, ranked candidates, and review labels, its schema or tool version, observation point, event and availability times, and missing-value behavior.
  3. Build a negative control. Include households, corporate networks, shared devices, merchants, schools, and public Wi-Fi form dense legitimate components and state the response it should receive.
  4. Replay and decide. Recompute from immutable input, compare expected output, inspect false positives and false negatives, then apply rank for asynchronous review, report precision at K and misses, inspect every lookalike, and block only when independent account-level evidence supports it.

Practice

Detection-engineering scenarios

BASELINE

1. The observed distribution changes

Trigger: a monitor for Link accounts/devices/IPs/payments through the Week-36 confidence-scored temporal entity links shifts outside its recorded baseline. Artifact: a sanitized graph with typed nodes, temporal/confidence edges, planted rings, planted legitimate shared-identifier components, ranked candidates, and review labels. Reasoning: compare time, producer, release, population, and data quality before attributing the shift to abuse. Boundary: a changed distribution is an observed fact, not a malicious label.

LOOKALIKE

2. A legitimate journey matches the candidate

Trigger: the candidate logic fires on households, corporate networks, shared devices, merchants, schools, and public Wi-Fi form dense legitimate components. Artifact: a labeled negative-control replay. Reasoning: preserve the case, inspect which feature dominated, and choose a proportionate fallback. Boundary: do not tune the case away without measuring what new false negatives the change creates.

QUALITY

3. The pipeline changes while the rule stays green

Trigger: unconditional entity merges, timeless edges, degree leakage, reviewing only planted rings, and blocking an entire component turn correlation into collective punishment. Artifact: contract-test result, schema versions, and quality counters. Reasoning: quarantine or mark degraded evidence and restore the producer contract before judging detection quality. Boundary: absence, default values, and parser failures are not benign observations.

POLICY

4. Evidence is strong enough to review, not to punish

Trigger: several versioned signals agree on an unusual owned-lab journey. Artifact: feature lineage, reason codes, policy version, and expected guardrails. Reasoning: rank for asynchronous review, report precision at K and misses, inspect every lookalike, and block only when independent account-level evidence supports it. Boundary: preserve rollback and never infer that an offline score proves the causal benefit of an intervention.
