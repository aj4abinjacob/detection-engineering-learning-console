Why it matters

IP is a network locator at an observation time. Detection uses it for rate, reputation, and routing context, never as sufficient human/account attribution.

IPv4 and CIDR

198.51.100.8  in 198.51.100.0/24  → yes (last 8 bits are host bits)
198.51.101.8  in 198.51.100.0/24  → no
203.0.113.63  in 203.0.113.0/26   → yes (range .0 through .63)

A prefix fixes its leftmost bits: /24 leaves 8 host bits (256 addresses), while /26 leaves 6 (64 addresses). Network/broadcast usability rules depend on context, so membership and assignability are separate questions. Prefixes represent contiguous ranges; smaller prefix length means a larger network. Predict first, then use ipaddress.ip_network to verify rather than treating mental arithmetic as evidence.

Public, private, and special-use

RFC 1918 private IPv4 ranges are 10/8, 172.16/12, and 192.168/16. 100.64/10 is shared address space for CGNAT, not ordinary private address space. 192.0.2/24, 198.51.100/24, and 203.0.113/24 are documentation ranges. Special-use classification evolves; consult IANA for current allocations.

Routing and forwarding

Destination            Next hop
0.0.0.0/0              192.0.2.1
198.51.100.0/24        192.0.2.2
198.51.100.128/25      192.0.2.3

198.51.100.42  → /24 via 192.0.2.2
198.51.100.200 → /25 via 192.0.2.3 (longest matching prefix)

For each destination, compare matching prefixes and choose the most specific; a route entry says a next hop/interface, not that the destination is responsive. Hosts send non-local destinations to a gateway; routers decrement TTL and forward by destination prefix. TTL limits loops, but traceroute hops are ICMP responses to deliberately expiring probes, not a complete ownership map. Asymmetric paths and filtering are common.

NAT and shared clients

NAT translates address/port mappings; many devices can share one public IP. CGNAT and corporate egress make one IP represent many unrelated users. Reverse proxies add another address boundary; only trusted forwarding metadata can recover a client claim.

Autonomous systems

An ASN identifies a routing domain that originates/announces prefixes through BGP policy. It can support coarse reputation or hosting/residential context, not a claim about operator intent, exact company, or end user. Public ASN lookups are current/network-dependent: save timestamp, source, prefix, and caveats.

Safe local calculation

python3 -c 'import ipaddress; print(ipaddress.ip_address("198.51.100.8") in ipaddress.ip_network("198.51.100.0/24"))'

This calculates membership locally. Use when validating fixtures; it proves syntax/range relation, not route reachability, public allocation, or identity.

Safety and platform limits

Do not probe or lookup targets for this guide. Use documentation IPs and your own authorized context. Routing tables, public ASN data, NAT behavior, and address ownership change; avoid treating stale lookup output as durable fact.

Detection scenarios

Rate alert on office egress

Shared corporate NAT can explain many accounts from one IP; add account/device/time evidence.

Private source reaches collector

It may be an internal address or malformed/proxy telemetry; establish collector path before blocking.

ASN reputation change

Record timestamp/prefix/source; routing announcements change and do not prove abuse.

CGNAT false positive

100.64/10 indicates shared-address context, not a unique household.
