Why it matters

Protocol serialization choices can reflect implementation families, but intermediaries and normal updates alter them. A signal proves an observed representation at one collector, not the endpoint identity or intent.

Capture and termination point

At passive edge, conventional ClientHello may be visible but HTTP under TLS is encrypted. At TLS termination, HTTP semantics are visible after decryption. A CDN/proxy can originate a different upstream TLS/HTTP connection, so label observer and peer for every field.

TLS ordered fields

Cipher suite and extension order, supported groups, key shares, signature algorithms, ALPN, SNI where visible, and GREASE are offered metadata. Preserve raw ordered values, parser version, normalization, and timestamp; they do not prove selection or application behavior.

JA3 and JA4

JA3 preserves ordered decimal lists from five ClientHello fields, removes GREASE under its method, joins a canonical string, and uses MD5 as a lookup key—not a trust primitive. The FoxIO TLS-client JA4 method uses structured a_b_c output, transport context, sorted cipher/extension inputs, and signature-algorithm information. Its exact current format/version comes from the official repository. Record the method-specific license: TLS-client JA4 is currently BSD 3-Clause while other JA4+ methods have separate terms. Do not invent formulas or call either universal identity.

HTTP/2 fingerprints

HTTP/2 can expose SETTINGS identifiers/order/values, initial flow-control window behavior, stream behavior, and pseudo-header ordering at the HTTP/2 endpoint. HPACK and proxy translation mean capture point/version matters. HTTP/2 settings are negotiation/performance metadata, not a user identity.

HTTP header order and consistency

Header order/casing, presence, values, Accept-Language, User-Agent, client hints, cookies, and declared protocol can be compared for internal consistency. HTTP specifications generally do not make ordinary header order semantic; intermediaries/frameworks can reorder/normalize them.

Consistency is an investigation feature

A mismatch among claimed user agent, TLS family, HTTP/2 behavior, browser signals, and journey may justify more evidence or a proportional challenge. It is not proof of proxy, automation, OS, or maliciousness; legitimate SDKs, accessibility, enterprise middleware, and updates create mismatches.

ECH and intermediaries

ECH can hide inner ClientHello name information where deployed/configured. TLS terminators, CDNs, forward proxies, HTTP translators, and load balancers can alter observed TLS/HTTP fingerprints. Record the actual TCP peer and possible intermediary before inference.

Safety, version, and license limits

Use sanitized owned PCAP/corpus or local AbuseGuard only. No vendor probing/bypass/evasion. Fingerprints vary/collide/mimic; privacy policy limits collection. Record JA4 FoxIO license/version and each parser/library version. Do not retain headers/cookies/SNI beyond authorized handling.

Defensive scenarios

New JA3 hash

Trigger: unseen hash. Artifact: raw canonical tuple/parser version. Observation: browser release timing. Reasoning: likely drift. Limit: hash cannot identify owner.

HTTP/2 settings mismatch

Trigger: changed SETTINGS. Artifact: termination-point trace. Observation: proxy upgrade. Reasoning: intermediary transformed peer. Limit: settings do not prove automation.

Header inconsistency

Trigger: claimed browser/header pattern differs. Artifact: sanitized header schema. Observation: enterprise gateway. Reasoning: request corroboration. Limit: accessibility/SDK is legitimate.

SNI coverage loss

Trigger: missing expected name. Artifact: collector/ECH state. Observation: encrypted hello rollout. Reasoning: visibility change. Limit: does not mean no traffic.
