Purpose

Why this matters in detection engineering

A decision contract is the stable boundary between detection and response: score or class, reason codes, evidence references, policy version, action, expiry, and trace identifier. Reason codes explain owned logic without exposing raw sensitive data.

A detection engineer must be able to trace how an observation becomes a feature, how that feature supports an inference, and how policy turns the inference into an action. The useful unit for this topic is a schema-validated decision with deterministic ordered reasons and an action chosen from an approved policy vocabulary. Preserve enough lineage to reproduce it without collecting content that the decision does not require.

Boundaries

What can go wrong

Closest legitimate lookalike: the same evidence may warrant observe, review, step-up, throttle, cached response, or block in different business contexts.

Pipeline or interpretation failure: free-text reasons, raw feature dumps, unbounded delay, or covertly punitive degradation make decisions unstable or unsafe.

Safety boundary: work only with supplied sanitized fixtures or systems and accounts you own and are authorized to test. Minimize identifiers, exclude secrets and content, bound retention and access, and provide an accessible path when an automated action could affect a person.

Do not overclaimThis guide establishes a method for controlled defensive evidence. It does not prove identity, intent, causality, universal tool behavior, or how a third-party system will classify traffic.

Method

A repeatable defensive workflow

  1. Declare the question and scope. Name the owned route, entity, time range, authorization, expected population, and prohibited data before collection.
  2. Version the evidence. Save a schema-validated decision with deterministic ordered reasons and an action chosen from an approved policy vocabulary, its schema or tool version, observation point, event and availability times, and missing-value behavior.
  3. Build a negative control. Include the same evidence may warrant observe, review, step-up, throttle, cached response, or block in different business contexts and state the response it should receive.
  4. Replay and decide. Recompute from immutable input, compare expected output, inspect false positives and false negatives, then apply keep reasons owner-facing, bound every action, preserve accessibility, and never use deceptive responses to cause harm.

Practice

Detection-engineering scenarios

BASELINE

1. The observed distribution changes

Trigger: a monitor for Contract: score + reasons + action shifts outside its recorded baseline. Artifact: a schema-validated decision with deterministic ordered reasons and an action chosen from an approved policy vocabulary. Reasoning: compare time, producer, release, population, and data quality before attributing the shift to abuse. Boundary: a changed distribution is an observed fact, not a malicious label.

LOOKALIKE

2. A legitimate journey matches the candidate

Trigger: the candidate logic fires on the same evidence may warrant observe, review, step-up, throttle, cached response, or block in different business contexts. Artifact: a labeled negative-control replay. Reasoning: preserve the case, inspect which feature dominated, and choose a proportionate fallback. Boundary: do not tune the case away without measuring what new false negatives the change creates.

QUALITY

3. The pipeline changes while the rule stays green

Trigger: free-text reasons, raw feature dumps, unbounded delay, or covertly punitive degradation make decisions unstable or unsafe. Artifact: contract-test result, schema versions, and quality counters. Reasoning: quarantine or mark degraded evidence and restore the producer contract before judging detection quality. Boundary: absence, default values, and parser failures are not benign observations.

POLICY

4. Evidence is strong enough to review, not to punish

Trigger: several versioned signals agree on an unusual owned-lab journey. Artifact: feature lineage, reason codes, policy version, and expected guardrails. Reasoning: keep reasons owner-facing, bound every action, preserve accessibility, and never use deceptive responses to cause harm. Boundary: preserve rollback and never infer that an offline score proves the causal benefit of an intervention.
