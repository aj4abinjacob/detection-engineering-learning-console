Purpose

Why this matters in detection engineering

Rules and machine learning are alternative decision systems that must be compared on the same temporally and entity-correct held-out events, action costs, latency budget, coverage, and operational burden. Complexity is not evidence of improvement.

A detection engineer must be able to trace how an observation becomes a feature, how that feature supports an inference, and how policy turns the inference into an action. The useful unit for this topic is a head-to-head report containing identical inputs, thresholds selected on validation data, error review, latency, reason quality, and ship decision. Preserve enough lineage to reproduce it without collecting content that the decision does not require.

Boundaries

What can go wrong

Closest legitimate lookalike: rules may be more stable and explainable while a model gains only on a narrow or immature subset.

Pipeline or interpretation failure: giving the model richer features, retuning on test data, ignoring abstentions, or omitting maintenance cost makes the comparison unfair.

Safety boundary: work only with supplied sanitized fixtures or systems and accounts you own and are authorized to test. Minimize identifiers, exclude secrets and content, bound retention and access, and provide an accessible path when an automated action could affect a person.

Do not overclaimThis guide establishes a method for controlled defensive evidence. It does not prove identity, intent, causality, universal tool behavior, or how a third-party system will classify traffic.

Method

A repeatable defensive workflow

  1. Declare the question and scope. Name the owned route, entity, time range, authorization, expected population, and prohibited data before collection.
  2. Version the evidence. Save a head-to-head report containing identical inputs, thresholds selected on validation data, error review, latency, reason quality, and ship decision, its schema or tool version, observation point, event and availability times, and missing-value behavior.
  3. Build a negative control. Include rules may be more stable and explainable while a model gains only on a narrow or immature subset and state the response it should receive.
  4. Replay and decide. Recompute from immutable input, compare expected output, inspect false positives and false negatives, then apply publish a ship or do-not-ship decision with the smallest system that meets cost, reliability, and explanation requirements.

Detection-engineering scenarios

BASELINE

1. The observed distribution changes

Trigger: a monitor for Identical held-out traffic + cost assumptions shifts outside its recorded baseline. Artifact: a head-to-head report containing identical inputs, thresholds selected on validation data, error review, latency, reason quality, and ship decision. Reasoning: compare time, producer, release, population, and data quality before attributing the shift to abuse. Boundary: a changed distribution is an observed fact, not a malicious label.

LOOKALIKE

2. A legitimate journey matches the candidate

Trigger: the candidate logic fires on rules may be more stable and explainable while a model gains only on a narrow or immature subset. Artifact: a labeled negative-control replay. Reasoning: preserve the case, inspect which feature dominated, and choose a proportionate fallback. Boundary: do not tune the case away without measuring what new false negatives the change creates.

QUALITY

3. The pipeline changes while the rule stays green

Trigger: giving the model richer features, retuning on test data, ignoring abstentions, or omitting maintenance cost makes the comparison unfair. Artifact: contract-test result, schema versions, and quality counters. Reasoning: quarantine or mark degraded evidence and restore the producer contract before judging detection quality. Boundary: absence, default values, and parser failures are not benign observations.

POLICY

4. Evidence is strong enough to review, not to punish

Trigger: several versioned signals agree on an unusual owned-lab journey. Artifact: feature lineage, reason codes, policy version, and expected guardrails. Reasoning: publish a ship or do-not-ship decision with the smallest system that meets cost, reliability, and explanation requirements. Boundary: preserve rollback and never infer that an offline score proves the causal benefit of an intervention.
