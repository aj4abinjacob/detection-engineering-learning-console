The defensible Week-0 answer

Large anti-abuse systems do not “know” a visitor is a bot from one fingerprint. They collect permitted signals at multiple points, normalize them, compare them to observed baselines/reputation, apply policy/risk scoring, and choose an outcome while managing false positives.

Full detection stack

network → TLS → HTTP → browser → behavioral → reputation → policy outcome

Each layer has distinct collectors, encryption boundaries, and failure modes. A CDN may only see edge connection facts while origin sees app/account facts; a browser challenge can add client evidence unavailable to a server-only API.

Defense in depth

Fusion means independent or partially independent evidence is assessed with confidence, recency, and context. A rate burst plus new account plus unusual request sequence is stronger than any part alone, but still a lead requiring proportional response and appeal/observability paths.

Network layer

IP, prefix, ASN, network category, geotemporal stability, rate, and distribution can establish connection/routing context at a time. NAT, mobile networks, VPNs, proxies, IPv6 privacy addresses, and stale reputation limit attribution.

TLS layer

ClientHello/JA3/JA4-style fields, cipher/extension order, and termination point can support implementation correlation. ECH, intermediaries, version changes, GREASE, collisions, and mimicry mean these are not identity.

HTTP layer

Method/path/header order/consistency, cookies, protocol version, and response behavior are observed at proxy/app. HTTP/2 settings/window/pseudo-header order may be edge-specific; proxies can normalize/replace fields.

Browser layer

Client-executed capability/consistency signals can supplement server observations, subject to privacy, accessibility, consent, and browser variation. They should not be silently treated as definitive identity claims.

Behavioral layer

Sequence, timing, velocity, navigation state, resource access, and account lifecycle can reveal automation-like patterns. Power users, assistive technology, scripts, QA, and incidents produce legitimate lookalikes.

Reputation and feedback

Historical abusive signals, verified abuse reports, and challenge outcomes need source, observation time, expiration, policy, and appeal/feedback loop. Reputation can be stale, poisoned, biased, or wrongly inherited through shared infrastructure.

Evidence limits and safety

Use only owned/sanitized corpora. Do not probe vendors or teach bypass/evasion. Record collector/termination point, schema/version, timestamps, missing data, and false-positive alternatives. Privacy and legal policy constrain collection and retention.

Defensive scenarios

Login burst

Trigger: rate jump. Artifact: time-bucket/account/IP table. Observation: shared ASN and normal browser mix. Reasoning: rate alone weak. Limit: campaign or office NAT may be legitimate.

New TLS pattern

Trigger: unknown fingerprint. Artifact: raw/normalized hello record. Observation: rollout coincides with browser update. Reasoning: version drift. Limit: cannot identify client owner.

Checkout automation

Trigger: repeated sequence. Artifact: session journey timeline. Observation: missing normal state transitions. Reasoning: multiple layers align. Limit: API/assistive client may differ.

Reputation block

Trigger: inherited bad IP score. Artifact: score source/time/TTL. Observation: residential shared address. Reasoning: reduce confidence. Limit: score cannot prove present abuse.
